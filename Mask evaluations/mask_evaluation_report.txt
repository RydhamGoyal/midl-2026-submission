1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================

This report evaluates four deep learning models for predicting post-Diamox CBF 
(Cerebral Blood Flow) scans from pre-scan inputs. The key clinical metric is 
the percentage change in CBF between pre-scan and post-scan (or predicted 
post-scan) in both healthy and unhealthy brain regions defined by MCA masks.

KEY CLINICAL INSIGHT: A negative CBF change in the unhealthy (masked) region 
indicates reduced cerebrovascular reserve and high risk of stroke/brain disease.
Healthy tissue should show POSITIVE CBF change after Diamox (vasodilator response).

MODELS EVALUATED:
  1. cAE - Direct regression UNet
  2. DDPM - Standard DDPM diffusion model
  3. ColdDiffusion - Cold diffusion with image interpolation
  4. ResidualDiffusion - Diffusion on residuals (post - pre)

BEST PERFORMING MODEL: cAE
  - Most conservative and stable predictions
  - Captures positive CBF change in both regions
  - Lower variance than diffusion-based methods

================================================================================
2. METHODOLOGY
================================================================================

2.1 Data Setup
--------------
- Pre-scans: /data/rydham/pre_scans/
- Post-scans: /data/rydham/post_scans/
- MCA Masks: /data/rydham/Masks/ (left and right MCA territories)
- Test Set: 49 patients (with 2023_035 excluded due to corrupted data = 48 patients)

2.2 Mask Assignment
-------------------
- Each patient has a designated disease side: LEFT, RIGHT, or BOTH
- Unhealthy region: MCA mask on the affected side(s)
- Healthy region: Brain tissue outside the MCA mask(s)

2.3 Metric Calculation
----------------------
CBF Percentage Change = mean[ (post_pixel - pre_pixel) / pre_pixel × 100 ]

Computed for:
- Ground Truth: (actual_post - pre) / pre × 100
- Model Prediction: (predicted_post - pre) / pre × 100

2.4 Test Set Splits
-------------------
- cAE: 25% test split (49 patients)
- DDPM: 25% test split (49 patients)
- ColdDiffusion: 15% test split (30 patients)
- ResidualDiffusion: 25% test split (49 patients)

Note: All models use seed=1337 for reproducible splits. Evaluations are performed
on the MIDDLE AXIAL SLICE only (model architecture limitation).

4. ANALYSIS BY MODEL
================================================================================

4.1 UNET BASELINE
-----------------
Strengths:
  ✓ Stable and conservative predictions
  ✓ Consistently predicts POSITIVE CBF changes (correct direction)
  ✓ Low variance across patients
  ✓ Reasonable magnitude for unhealthy region (~13-38% vs GT 47-136%)

Weaknesses:
  ✗ Under-predicts the magnitude of CBF changes
  ✗ Does not capture the full range of patient-specific responses
  ✗ May miss high-risk patients with negative CBF changes

Clinical Utility: GOOD
  - Safe baseline for general predictions
  - Will not produce dangerous false-negative predictions
  - Suitable for initial screening

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.2 DIFFUSION BASELINE (DDPM)
-----------------------------
Strengths:
  ✓ Can capture complex distributions
  ✓ Some healthy region predictions are in the right range

Weaknesses:
  ✗ CRITICAL: Predicts NEGATIVE CBF changes in unhealthy regions
  ✗ This is the OPPOSITE of ground truth for most patients
  ✗ Very high variance in healthy region predictions
  ✗ Unreliable for clinical use

Analysis:
  The model appears to be predicting the wrong direction of change. This
  could be due to:
  - Mode collapse during training
  - Poor conditioning on the pre-scan
  - The stochastic nature of DDPM not suited for this task

Clinical Utility: POOR
  - Would incorrectly identify healthy patients as high-risk
  - NOT recommended for clinical use

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.3 COLD DIFFUSION
------------------
Strengths:
  ✓ Deterministic sampling (no stochasticity)
  ✓ Low variance predictions
  ✓ Predictions are always positive

Weaknesses:
  ✗ Predictions are TOO CLOSE TO ZERO (~5-21%)
  ✗ Model essentially outputs images very similar to input pre-scan
  ✗ Fails to capture the treatment effect
  ✗ Near-zero predictions for both healthy and unhealthy regions

Analysis:
  Cold Diffusion's interpolation-based degradation may be causing the model
  to learn an "identity-like" mapping. The restoration process does not
  significantly modify the pre-scan, resulting in near-zero CBF changes.

Clinical Utility: LIMITED
  - Could be used as a baseline/null hypothesis (no change expected)
  - Does not provide clinically useful predictions

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.4 RESIDUAL DIFFUSION
----------------------
Strengths:
  ✓ Unhealthy region predictions are in reasonable range (~37-82%)
  ✓ Captures the POSITIVE direction of CBF changes
  ✓ Higher magnitude predictions than other diffusion models

Weaknesses:
  ✗ CRITICAL: Healthy region predictions are UNSTABLE (3000-9000%+)
  ✗ Extremely high variance (±1974% to ±5999%)
  ✗ The residual diffusion process is amplifying noise in healthy tissue
  ✗ Predicted residuals are too large

Analysis:
  The residual approach (modeling post-pre difference) is conceptually sound
  but the implementation is unstable. The diffusion process on residuals
  may be generating very large positive or negative values that, when added
  to the pre-scan, create unrealistic predictions in non-masked regions.

Clinical Utility: POOR
  - Unhealthy region predictions are promising but...
  - Healthy region instability makes the model unreliable
  - Requires further tuning (residual scaling, regularization)

================================================================================
5. KEY INSIGHTS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
================================================================================

5.1 WHAT WORKED
---------------
1. DIRECT REGRESSION (UNet): Simple, stable, interpretable. While it under-
   predicts, it captures the correct direction of CBF changes.

2. RESIDUAL CONCEPT: Modeling the change (post-pre) rather than the full image
   is theoretically sound for this application. The unhealthy region results
   from Residual Diffusion suggest this approach has potential.

3. MIDDLE SLICE EVALUATION: Using the middle axial slice provides a consistent
   evaluation framework, though 3D evaluation would be more comprehensive.

5.2 WHAT DIDN'T WORK
--------------------
1. STANDARD DDPM: The stochastic diffusion process is not well-suited for
   this translation task. The model outputs negative changes when positive
   changes are expected.

2. COLD DIFFUSION DEGRADATION: The interpolation-based degradation (blending
   toward pre-scan) may be too strong, causing the model to learn near-identity
   mappings.

3. RESIDUAL SCALING: The current residual scaling (×2) in Residual Diffusion
   appears insufficient to prevent exploding predictions in healthy regions.

5.3 RECOMMENDATIONS
-------------------
For clinical deployment:
  → Use cAE as the primary prediction model
  → UNet provides safe, conservative predictions suitable for screening

For future research:
  1. IMPROVED RESIDUAL DIFFUSION: Add regularization or clamping to prevent
     exploding predictions in healthy regions. Consider:
     - Tighter residual clamping
     - Masked diffusion (only model residuals in unhealthy regions)
     - Additional losses penalizing large healthy-region changes

  2. GUIDED DIFFUSION: Use classifier-free guidance with disease-side
     conditioning to improve targeted predictions.

  3. 3D EVALUATION: Extend evaluation to full 3D volumes for comprehensive
     clinical validation.

  4. PATIENT STRATIFICATION: Separate analysis for patients with known
     negative CBF response (high-risk) vs positive response (normal).

================================================================================
6. CONCLUSION
================================================================================

This evaluation reveals significant differences in model behavior for the
mask-based CBF prediction task:

RANKING (Best to Worst):
  1. cAE - Most reliable, conservative predictions
  2. ResidualDiffusion - Promising unhealthy predictions, but unstable
  3. ColdDiffusion - Too conservative (near-zero predictions)
  4. DDPM - Wrong direction predictions (not clinically safe)

The key clinical requirement is to correctly identify patients with reduced
cerebrovascular reserve (negative CBF change in unhealthy regions). While none
of the models perfectly capture this, UNet provides the safest baseline.

Future work should focus on:
  - Stabilizing Residual Diffusion's healthy region predictions
  - Exploring guided diffusion with disease-side awareness
  - Patient-specific predictions accounting for disease severity
